\begin{definition}[horizontal asymptote]
  If a constant \(L\) satisfies 
  \[
    L = \lim_{x \to \infty} f(x) \quad\text{or}\quad L = \lim_{x \to -\infty} f(x),
  \]
  then the horizontal line \(y = L\) is called a \hlmain{horizontal asymptote} for \(f(x)\).

  \textbook{page 408}
\end{definition}
The quickest way to determine horizontal asymptotes of transcendental functions (trigs, inverse trigs, \(e^{x}\) and \(\ln(x)\)) is by graphing them.

We now evaluate limits at infinity.  We need the following facts coming from relevant graphs.

\begin{mdframed}[style=simple-compact]

  \begin{align*}
    \lim_{x \to \infty} e^{x} &= \infty, & \lim_{x \to \infty} x^{(\text{positive constant})} &= \infty, & \quad \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{1}{x^{(\text{negative constant})}} &= \infty \\
    \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{1}{e^{x}} &= 0, & \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{1}{x^{(\text{positive constant})}} &= 0, & \quad \lim_{x \to \infty} x^{(\text{negative constant})} &= 0 \\
                                        && \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{e^{x}}{x^{\text{(positive constant)}}} &= \infty & \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{x^{\text{(positive constant)}}}{e^{x}} &= 0.
  \end{align*}

  Moreover, 
  \(
  \lim_{x \to \infty} (\text{polynomial}) = (\text{the sign of the coefficient for \(x^{\text{largest power}}\)}) \, \infty
  \)
\end{mdframed}

All limit laws apply to limits at infinity, but we often need to reorganize functions by \hlmain{factoring out its dominant (fastest growing) terms}. This technique is called \hlmain{analyzing dominant terms}. There are three possible scenarios as demonstrated by Examples~\ref{eg:dom-term-intro-constant}, \ref{eg:dom-term-intro-zero} and \ref{eg:dom-term-intro-infinity}.

\faStar{} A \hlmain{change of variable} \(u = -x\) turns limits at negative infinity to limits at positive infinity:
\[
  \lim_{x \to -\infty} f(x) = \lim_{u \to \infty} f(-u).
\]
This little trick is not in the textbook but helps avoid subtle sign mistakes while analyzing dominant terms at \(-\infty\), especially for algebraic functions with even roots. 


\begin{example}
  Evaluate \(\lim_{x \to -\infty} \frac{\sqrt{x^{2} + 1}}{x + 1}\). 

  \blanklines{25}
\end{example}
