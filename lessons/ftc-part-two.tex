\begin{document} \section{The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, part 2}
The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus also makes a connection between definite and indefinite integrals. Before we dive into the abstract statement, think of velocity and position functions again. The following statement is true.
\[
  \int_{a}^{b} \left(\text{velocity at time \(t\)}\right) \;dt  = (\text{position at time \(b\)}) - (\text{position at time \(a\)})
\]
\blanks{3}

\begin{thm} \label{thm:ftc-part-two}
  If \(f(x)\) is continuous over \([a,b]\) and \(F(x)\) is any antiderivative of \(f(x)\), then
  \[
    \int_{a}^{b} f(x) \;dx = F(b) - F(a).
  \]

  \textbook{Theorem 5.5: The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus Part 2 on page 555}
\end{thm}

FTC part 2 is our \hlmain{workhorse theorem} to evaluate definite integrals. \hlmain{Indefinite integrals and all associated integration formulas} can now be used to evaluate definite integrals.

The properties of indefinite integrals carry over to definite ones.
\begin{thm}
  \begin{align*}
    \int_{a}^{b} (\text{constant}) f(x) \;dx &= (\text{constant}) \int_{a}^{b} f(x) \;dx && \text{(constant multiple)}\\[1ex]
    \int_{a}^{b} f(x) \;dx + \int_{a}^{b} g(x) \;dx &= \int_{a}^{b} f(x) + g(x) \;dx && \text{(sum)} \\[1ex]
    \int_{a}^{b} f(x) \;dx - \int_{a}^{b} g(x) \;dx &= \int_{a}^{b} f(x) - g(x) \;dx && \text{(difference)}
  \end{align*}

  \textbook{Equations (5.13), (5.11) and (5.12) on page 538}
\end{thm}
