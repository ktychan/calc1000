\begin{document} \section{The chain rule\texorpdfstring{ \((f \circ g)'(x) = f'(g(x))g'(x)\)}{}}

This lesson is about developing procedural proficiency for the chain rule.

\begin{mdframed}[style=simple-compact] \label{thm:diff-rules-3}
  \textbf{Differentiation Rules} \hfill {\footnotesize (part \(3\) of \(3\))}

  The derivative of a composite function \(f(g(x))\), if it is well-defined, can be calculated by the formula
  \begin{align*}
    \frac{d}{dx} f\big( g(x) \big)
    &= f'(g(x)) g'(x)
    &&\text{(the chain rule)}\\[1em]
    \frac{dy}{dx} &= \frac{dy}{du} \frac{du}{dx} \text{ if we write } y = f(u), u = g(x).
  \end{align*}
\end{mdframed}

\faStar{} The chain rule is required whenever we differentiate a composite function.

  Let \(f(x) = 10^x\). Find \(f''(x)\).

\end{example}
