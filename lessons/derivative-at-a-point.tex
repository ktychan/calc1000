The graphs of \(f(x)\) and \(g(x)\) are \emph{qualitatively} different at the marked point.
\begin{itemize}
  \item The graph of \(f(x)\) near the marked point appears to be \underline{\hspace{1in}} if we zoom in enough.
  \item The graph of \(g(x)\) near the marked point does not appear to be \underline{\hspace{1in}} no matter how much we zoom in.
\end{itemize}

The derivative quantifies this qualitative difference.  

\begin{definition} \label{def:derivative-at-a-point}
  Let \(f(x)\) be a function defined on an \underline{\hspace{1in}} interval containing some constant \(a\). The \hlmain{derivative of \(f(x)\) at \(a\)}, denoted by \hlmain{\(f'(a)\)} is defined by
  \begin{align} 
    \label{eq:def-derivative-1}
    f'(a) &= \phantom{\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(a + h) - f(a)}{h}}\\
    \intertext{or equivalently}
    \label{eq:def-derivative-2}
    f'(a) &= \phantom{\lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x) - f(a)}{x - a}}
  \end{align}
  provided that \underline{\hspace{2in}}.

  The \hlmain{tangent line for \(f(x)\) at \(x = a\)} is the line with slope \underline{\hspace{2cm}} and passes through the point \underline{\hspace{3cm}}. Its equation is
  \[
    y = \phantom{f'(a) (x - a) + f(a)} \hspace{1in}
  \]

  \textbook{page 217}
\end{definition}

\faStar{} Geometrically, \(f'(a)\) represents \underline{\hspace{4in}}.

Terminology: If \(f'(a)\) exists, then \(f\) is said to be \hlmain{differentiable at \(a\)}, and \(f(x)\) has a tangent line at \(x = a\). If \(f'(a)\) does not exist, then \(f\) is said to be \hlmain{not differentiable at \(a\)}, and \(f(x)\) does not have a tangent line at \(x = a\).
\clearpage

The limit definition of derivatives, e.g. Equation~\eqref{eq:def-derivative-1} or \eqref{eq:def-derivative-2}, is fundamentally important and \hlmain{useful for unfamiliar problems}. If you know differentiation rules, the exercises in this section may seem overly complicated. However, it is the purpose of this section to help you understand that, fundamentally, the derivative is a limit. Everything you know about limits can be used to solve differentiation problems, especially the unfamiliar ones.  \label{comment:derivative-definition}

  Find the derivative of \(f(x) = \sqrt{x}\) at \(4\) using Equation~\eqref{eq:def-derivative-1} or \eqref{eq:def-derivative-2}. Write down the equation of the tangent line at \(x = 4\).
