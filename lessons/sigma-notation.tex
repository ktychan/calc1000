\begin{document} \section{The Sigma notation}
The Sigma notation is a compact way to express the sum of a bunch of numbers and is mainly used in \thiscourse{} to express and manipulate Riemann sums (See Definitions~\ref{def:riemann-sum}~and~\ref{def:definite-integral}).  It is \hlmain{important} that you can recognize the expression in Definition~\ref{def:riemann-sum}.  Therefore, the entire purpose of this lecture is to help you feel comfortable with the Sigma notation. 

\blanklines{3}

{\huge \[ \sum_{i=m}^{n} a_{i} = \hspace{3in}\]}

\blanklines{3}

\begin{example} \label{ex:sigma-notation-read}
  Write the following summations in expanded form.
  \begin{align*}
    \sum_{i=3}^{5} (2i^{3} + 1)
      &= \underline{\hspace{5in}} \\[3ex]
      \sum_{k=10}^{13} x
      &= \underline{\hspace{5in}}.
  \end{align*}
\end{example}

When we need to express a sum using Sigma notation, remember that the index of summation, say \(i\), \hlwarn{must be an integer and increases by exactly \(1\)}.

\begin{thm}
  \label{page:summation-properties}
  Let \(c\) be a constant. Let \(a_{1}, \dots, a_{n}, b_{1}, \dots, b_{n}\) be two lists of numbers. 
  \begin{align*}
    \sum_{i=1}^{n} {\color{attn} \mathbf{c}} \; a_{i} 
      &= {\color{attn} \mathbf{c}} \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_{i} 
      &&\text{(constant multiple)} \\[6ex]
      \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_{i} {\color{attn}\,\mathbf{+}\,} \sum_{i=1}^{n} b_{i} 
      &= \sum_{i=1}^{n} (a_{i} {\color{attn}\,\mathbf{+}\,}\, b_{i}) 
      &&\text{(sum)} \\[6ex]
      \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_{i} {\color{attn}\,\mathbf{-}\,} \sum_{i=1}^{n} b_{i} 
      &= \sum_{i=1}^{n} (a_{i} {\color{attn}\,\mathbf{-}\,} b_{i})
      && \text{(difference)} \\[6ex]
      \sum_{i=1}^{{\color{attn}\mathbf{n}}} a_{i} 
      &= 
      \sum_{i=1}^{{\color{attn}\mathbf{m}}} a_{i} + 
      \sum_{i={\color{attn}\mathbf{m+1}}}^{{\color{attn}\mathbf{n}}} a_{i} 
      && \text{(split/merge a sum)} \\[6ex]
  \end{align*}

  \textbook{Rule: Properties of Sigma Notation, page 509}
\end{thm}

To \hlmain{simplify} or to \hlmain{evaluate} a summation written in Sigma notation means to perform the specified addition.  The formulas in Equation~\eqref{formula:summations} are useful and examinable.
\begin{equation} \label{formula:summations}
  \sum_{i=1}^{n} i = {\color{main} \frac{n(n+1)}{2}}, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^{n} i^{2} = {\color{main}\frac{n(n+1)}{2}} \cdot \frac{2n+1}{3}, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^{n} i^{3} = {\color{main}\frac{n(n+1)}{2}} \cdot \frac{n(n+1)}{2}.
\end{equation}
