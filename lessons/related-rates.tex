\begin{document} \section{Related rates}
Given a \hlmain{relation among two quantities}, we can use implicit differentiation \hlmain{with respect to time \(t\)} to find the \emph{relation} among rates of change with respect to time \(t\). \updatenote{"rates of changes" corrected to "rates of change".}

\begin{mdframed}[style=simple-compact]
  \textbf{Key principle}. \hlmain{Relate given quantities} (write down an equation) and do calculus. \updatenote{"Related" corrected to "Relate" (imperative, parallel with "do calculus").}
\end{mdframed}

\begin{example}[Textbook Example~4.2] \label{ex:related-rates-plane}
  An airplane is flying overhead at a constant elevation of \(4000\) ft. A height-less observer is viewing the plane from a position of \(3000\) ft from the base of a radio tower. The plane is flying horizontally away from the observer. If the plane is flying at the rate of \(600\) ft/sec, at what rate is the distance between the observer and the plane increasing when the plane passes over the radio tower?

  \begin{enumerate}[wide, label=(Step~\arabic*).]
    \item Introduce notations. Find an equation that relates all relevant quantities.

      \blanklines{10}

    \item State your goal: Find \underline{\hspace{1in}} given \underline{\hspace{3in}}.

    \item Find an expression for \(\frac{d}{dt}(\text{the desired quantity})\) using implicit differentiation.

      \blanklines{10}

    \item The expression for \(\frac{d}{dt}\left( \text{the desired quantity} \right)\) tells you exactly what you need to find to achieve your goal.

      \blanklines{10}
  \end{enumerate}
\end{example}

The examples and exercises in this section (up to now) could mislead us to believe that related rates problems are all about right-angle triangles. NOT always. Exercise~\ref{ex:related-rates-pyramids} does not use any right-angle triangles to set up the initial equation. \begin{exercise} \label{ex:related-rates-pyramids}
  An empty water tank is shaped like an upside-down pyramid, with base of \(3\) metres by \(3\) metres and a height of \(12\) metres. Water enters the tank at a rate of \(2\,\text{m}^{3}/\text{s}\) (cubic metres per second). How fast does the height of the water increase when the water is \(2\) metres deep? \updatenote{"2-metre deep" corrected to "2 metres deep".}

  The volume of a pyramid is \(\frac{(\text{area of the base}) \times (\text{height})}{3}\).

  \blanklines{45}
\end{exercise}
